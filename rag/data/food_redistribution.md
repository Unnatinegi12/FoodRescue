# Food Redistribution

Food redistribution connects organisations with surplus food to NGOs or community groups that can use it. Safety should remain the priority throughout the process.

## Donation information

A food donation should include useful information such as:

- Food type
- Quantity
- Preparation date and time
- Expiry or recommended use information when applicable
- Storage condition
- Donor information
- Allergen information when available

## Matching donations

When matching food with an NGO, consider:

- Whether the NGO accepts that type of food
- Quantity required by the NGO
- Storage capacity
- Location and transportation distance
- Remaining safe consumption time
- Dietary requirements

## Prioritising urgent donations

Food with a shorter remaining safe consumption period should generally be considered sooner, provided that it has remained safe and properly stored.

## Rejection conditions

An NGO should reject a donation if the food appears spoiled, contaminated, improperly stored, has damaged packaging, or has insufficient information to establish that it can be safely distributed.

## Traceability

FoodRescue should maintain records of donations, donors, receiving organisations and matching decisions. These records can help organisations understand where food came from and how it was redistributed.
