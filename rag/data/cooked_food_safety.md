# Cooked Food Safety

This note summarises common good practice for cooked food that may be donated. It is a learning resource, not legal advice. Always follow the rules of your local food-safety authority.

## The temperature danger zone

Bacteria multiply fastest between about 5 °C and 60 °C. Cooked food should be kept hot (above 60 °C) or cold (below 5 °C). Cooked food should not stay in the danger zone for more than 2 hours in total. In hot weather (above about 32 °C) the limit is 1 hour.

## Cooked rice

Cooked rice can carry Bacillus cereus spores that survive cooking. If rice is left warm, the spores grow and can produce toxins that reheating does not destroy. To redistribute cooked rice safely:

- Serve it hot, or cool it quickly by spreading it in shallow trays.
- Refrigerate within 1 hour of cooking and keep it below 5 °C.
- Donate it the same day. Do not keep it for more than about 1 day.
- Reheat only once, until steaming hot throughout (at least 74 °C).

## Other high-risk cooked foods

Dal, curries, gravies, cooked meat, fish, egg dishes, paneer and cooked vegetables are moist, protein-rich foods where bacteria grow quickly. Treat them as high-risk: keep them out of the danger zone and record the cooking time.

## Do not donate

Food that smells, looks or tastes unusual, food that has been served on a plate and returned, food that was left out at room temperature beyond the time limit, and food that has already been reheated once.
